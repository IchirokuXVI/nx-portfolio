Steppers

The stepper with buttons, D3, as the owner approved it on 2026-11-11, with today's control beside it. This page is the reference for the velista plans that build it: the stepper, the search at the top of a list, the shopping list's amount and its sheet, and the compact row. Every screen is the real page with only the named parts changed. The controls work: press them, hold them, and type in the sheets.

Decided, drawn on every screen

  • The stepper is minus, the amount over its unit, plus, with an outline. It is one size everywhere, and wider in the sheets.
  • The step is 25 g, 25 ml, and 1 for a count, 10 from 1 K, so a press always changes what you read. Plus then minus always returns to the same amount. Minus from less than one step goes to 0, and plus turns off when one more step would pass the most.
  • Holding minus or plus repeats, from slower to faster, up to ten steps a second. A hold stops before 0, because at 0 a line with nothing to keep is deleted; a separate press goes to 0. The app waits for the presses to stop before it saves.
  • The composer has no quantity stepper. You type and press add. A product not on the list is added at one step.
  • Amounts go up to 999 999 grams, millilitres or units. A row reads at most 999,99 kg; a sheet reads the exact 999,999 kg. Three decimals is the most there can be, because amounts are whole grams and millilitres. A unit can have up to four letters.
  • A row shows at most two decimals: 1,275 kg reads 1,28 kg. The sheets show the exact amount.
  • The amount is typed only in a line's sheet. In a row, a card, the product page and the line page it is an output, changed only by minus and plus.
  • The line page has the buttons and no typing.
  • The shopping list only shows the amount, in a cell of one size. Pressing it opens the row's sheet, where the amount is changed. The sheet swaps a product with the two arrow icon.
  • In the catalog, a product already on the list has its plus switched off, with a message and a link to the line. The product page draws no switched off plus: its table steps each list's line.
  • The search field sits directly under the page header of the list and of the shopping list. The progress bar is gone for now. "Por comprar" and "Ponerlas en orden" are gone. The bottom bar stays while typing, and the first drag closes the keyboard.
  • The compact row is an alternative view of the list, chosen in the settings. It has three lines of facts.

How an amount is written

At most three whole digits and two decimals, rounded half up for the display only. At 1000 the amount moves up a name: 999 g, then 1 kg. The sheets show the exact amount: 1,275 kg. A row never reads 1000 of the top name: from 999 995 g it reads 999,99 kg.

The step is 25 g, 25 ml, and 1 for a count, 10 from 1 K, so a press always changes what you read: a count at 1000 (1 K) goes to 1010 (1,01 K). Minus takes away the step plus uses one base step lower, so 1 K minus is 999 and plus then minus always returns to the same amount. The exact amount can be typed in the sheet.

The most is 999 999 grams, millilitres or units. The plus turns off when one more step would pass it; it never jumps to the most. Counting up from zero in grams, it stops at 999 975 g, which a row reads as 999,98 kg.

At the bottom, minus from an amount under one step goes to 0, and minus is off at 0.

Three decimals is the most a sheet can ever show, because an amount is stored in whole grams, millilitres or units.

In a sheet, a number typed with a decimal into grams, millilitres or a plain count is read one name up: 1,5 into 500 ml is 1,5 l, and 1,5 into a count of 2 is 1,5 K.

A single press steps once, on release. Holding minus or plus repeats after 400 ms: the first repeat comes 300 ms later, each next one a little sooner, up to ten steps a second. Lifting the finger, sliding off the button, a cancelled pointer or a scroll stops it. A hold on minus stops at the last amount above 0; a separate press goes to 0, because at 0 a line with nothing to keep is deleted. Nothing in the mock is saved. In the app, the feature's logic waits for the presses to stop before it sends the change; the stepper itself sends nothing.

What each look measures

Measured by script from the rendered frames as this page loads: A and D3 from their zone list screens, and the two sheet controls from their sheets.

1.4.11 asks 3 to 1 only of what is needed to identify the control. Here that is the glyphs and the amount, so an outline below 3 to 1, or no outline, still passes. The minus and plus stay in the tab order on purpose, for switch access and voice control. In a row the amount is read out with the group's name and cannot be focused; in a sheet it is a spinbutton, and the arrow keys also step.
