Scale

THE FOUR KINDS (backend 0244, section 1).

Fixed pack: a size, price is the pack. Unchanged.

Sold by the kilo: KILOGRAM, no size, no weight. Price is a kilo.

Kilo piece: KILOGRAM, no size, approxWeight in grams. Price is a kilo, piecePrice comes from the server.

Piece priced as one: UNIT, no size, approxWeight. Price is the piece, as a pack.

WHAT EACH PLACE SAYS.

The weight line: "~1,56 kg" or "~580 g", for any product with a weight, in the size's place, through Intl. Always with the tilde: the weight is an estimate, and the tilde is the sign the total already uses for one.

The headline: piecePrice for a kilo piece, "13,42 € / kg" for a product sold by the kilo, price for everything else.

The unit line: always on a kilo piece, also at exactly 1 kg. Never on a product sold by the kilo. Rule 5 of 0101 for a pack and for a piece priced as one.

Every "/ kg" and "/ L" is drawn smaller than its number, in the muted colour, close after it, wherever a price is drawn: the headline, the unit line, the basket caption and the price table.

The detail wraps at its separator when it does not fit beside a two line price, so the weight is never cut short. The brand gives way to a second line, the number does not.

THE TOTALS.

A list total and a basket total add piecePrice for a kilo piece and price for every other product. Two cheese pieces are 41,88 €.

A kilo piece whose offer has a price and no piecePrice adds nothing and counts as unpriced.

A loose product still adds quantity times the price per kilo. The owner set that aside on 2026-11-03 (0244, D4).

THE OWNER'S CHOICES, 2026-11-05.

1. The tilde, not "aprox.", on every weight, to save room.

2. "/ kg" smaller than its number everywhere. A product sold by the kilo took too much room and its number sat out of line with the other prices.

3. B for the headline of a product sold by the kilo, with less room on both sides of the slash.
