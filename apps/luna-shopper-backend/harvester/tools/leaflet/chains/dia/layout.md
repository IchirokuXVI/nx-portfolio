# What a Dia leaflet page looks like

Read this before the first three page images of a new Dia leaflet. If the
pages do not match this description, stop and report before reading any
further page: the reading process assumes this layout, and a page that breaks
it will misread quietly rather than loudly.

Written from two editions, the week of 02/09 to 08/09 and the week of 09/09 to
15/09, so it describes the format and not one issue. Both are twelve pages,
both are flattened artwork with no text layer on any page, and both lay a page
out the same way.

- **The page is tall and narrow**, one page and not a two page spread. There
  is no grid. Product photographs sit loosely on a themed background, each one
  with its own price badge beside it and a caption in bold dark text with the
  product name, then a lighter line with the brand and the size. A busy page
  holds ten to twenty offers. A feature page holds one, printed large under a
  slogan.
- **Every price sits inside a solid rectangular badge** with the number in
  large white figures. The badge is red on a white page and black on a
  coloured campaign page. The colour carries no meaning. There is no other
  place on the tile a price appears.
- **The badge price is one number split in two.** The euros are large and the
  cents are printed small and raised, with no comma between them. A large `0`
  with a raised `49` is 0,49. A large `1` with a raised `20` is 1,20. A badge
  with a large figure and no raised cents is a whole euro: `1` is 1,00 and `2`
  is 2,00.
- **The decimal separator everywhere else is a comma**: `1,23 €/kg`, `0,55€`,
  `2,85€`. Only the badge price uses the raised cents instead.
- **A small comparison figure sits inside the badge, to the right of the big
  number**, written as a number over a unit: `1,23 €/kg`, `0,46 €/l`,
  `1,06 €/ud.`, `20,83 €/kg`. It is never the product price.
- **A unit with no number of its own is the basis of the badge price, not a
  comparison.** `€/kg` alone beside the big number means the badge price buys
  one kilogram, and `€/ud.` or `/ud.` alone means it buys one unit.
- **A number printed sideways beside the badge is Dia's article reference**,
  four to six digits such as `326`, `50844`, `221537`, `1075977 - 47702`. It
  is not a price, not a size and not a promotion.
- **Club Dia is the loyalty mechanic, and both prices are printed.** A red
  rounded flash reading `con CLUB Dia` sits above the badge, next to a
  discount such as `-20%`, `-25%`, `-31%` or the words `tu precio`. When that
  flash is there the big badge number is the club member price, and a white
  strip under the badge prints `PVP NO SOCIO` with the non member price and
  its own comparison figure: `PVP NO SOCIO 1,50€ 4,29 €/kg`. So a club tile
  states two prices, and the `PVP NO SOCIO` one is what anybody pays.
- **Second unit and bulk offers print the single unit price too.** The flash
  reads `LA 2ª UD. -50%`, `LA 2ª UD. -70%`, `3x2`, `2x1€` or `3x1€`, usually
  beside the Club Dia flash. The badge then carries a caption above the big
  number, `La 2ª unidad te sale a:` or `Llevando 3 la ud. te sale a:`, and a
  strip under it reading `Llevando 1 ud.` with the plain single unit price and
  that price's own comparison figure. The badge number is what the second or
  the bulk unit costs. The `Llevando 1 ud.` number is what one unit costs on
  its own, with no card and no second unit.
- **A price drop prints the old price struck through under the badge**, with
  its own struck comparison figure: `0,55€` and `1,38 €/kg`. It is announced
  either by a `¡SUPER PRECIO!` banner across the top of the badge or by an
  `Ahora` flash with a percentage, `Ahora -20%`, `Ahora -34%`. A struck price
  and a `PVP NO SOCIO` price are different things and never appear together.
- **Fresh weighed goods carry their own label beside the badge.**
  `PVP BANDEJA APROXIMADO` means the badge price is for one tray and the tray
  weight is approximate. `PRECIO POR KG` means the badge price buys one
  kilogram.
- **There is no department heading banner.** Pages are themed, page two is
  tomato and sauces and page eleven is drinks and deodorant, but nothing on
  the page names a department. What a page does print instead is a campaign
  banner: `DIA WORLD BURGER TOUR`, `TU COMPRA AYUDA / COMPRA 1, DONAMOS 1`,
  `VIVE LA CARRERA EN MADRING`, `MUCHO SE ESTÁ HABLANDO DE ESTE NUEVO DIA`.
  Those name a campaign, not a department, and no product on the page belongs
  to them as a category.
- **Every page but the cover carries a header strip** with the page number,
  the validity as `DEL 02/09 AL 08/09` and the Dia logo. The cover prints the
  same date range at the top right. Both bounds are printed, as day and month
  with no year.
- **Page one is the cover and page twelve is the back cover**, and both carry
  priced products. There is no index page.
- **Plenty printed on a tile is not a promotion of this leaflet.** Packaging
  flashes on the photograph belong to the manufacturer's pack: `x8`, `x2`,
  `SIN GLUTEN`, `100% natural`, `SIN AZÚCARES AÑADIDOS`, `PREMIO SEGURO`,
  `FORMATO FAMILIAR`, a Michelin star seal, an origin seal. So does
  `PRODUCTOS combinables entre sí`, which says which tiles a mix and match
  offer covers. So do the QR codes, the app adverts, the social icons, the
  alcohol legal notice and the `Ofertas no acumulables con cupones descuento`
  footer that closes every page.
