You are reading ONE page of a Spanish supermarket promotional leaflet (folleto)
from the chain LIDL.

Return ONLY a JSON array of the product offers advertised on this page.
No prose, no explanation, no markdown code fence. Just the array.

The array elements are rows that will be imported into a product catalog, so
every element MUST have exactly these keys:

{
  "name": "product description as printed, without the price, the brand or the pack size",
  "brand": "manufacturer brand, or null",
  "unitSize": 0.0,
  "sizeFormat": "kg" | "g" | "l" | "ml" | "ud" | "m" | null,
  "price": 0.00,
  "unitPrice": 0.00,
  "unitPriceLabel": "the comparison figure, verbatim, or null",
  "category": "the kind of product in a word or two, or null",
  "categoryPath": ["BANNER HEADLINE AS PRINTED"],
  "leaflet": {
    "format": "the pack as printed, or null",
    "basis": "unit" | "pack" | "kg" | "l",
    "wasPrice": 0.00,
    "loyalty": false,
    "validUntil": "the offer's own last day as YYYY-MM-DD, or null",
    "validityText": "the offer's own date wording, verbatim, or null",
    "promotion": null,
    "doubt": "what you are unsure of on this tile, in one sentence, or null"
  }
}

"category" is optional free text: the kind of product in a word or two, in
Spanish, as the page or the pack names it ("Leche", "Cerveza", "Detergente").
It is not a fixed list of values. Write null when the page gives no clue.

"leaflet.promotion" is either null or an object:

{
  "type": "price_drop" | "second_unit_discount" | "multibuy_unit_price"
        | "multibuy_total" | "n_for_m" | "buy_n_get_free" | "pack_bonus",
  "rawText": "the offer wording exactly as the leaflet prints it",
  "requiredQuantity": 0,
  "effectiveUnitPrice": 0.00,
  "totalPrice": 0.00,
  "singleUnitPrice": 0.00
}

"rawText" is required whenever "promotion" is not null. A promotion with no
wording is kept, and the builder records a warning so a person reads it off
the page.

HOW THIS LEAFLET IS LAID OUT

The page is tall and narrow, cut into full bleed colour panels, one product
tile to a panel, two to eight tiles a page. A tile is a product name in bold,
a few lines of small text under it (the size, then the comparison figures),
and one very large number set straight into the panel with no badge around it.
That number is the price. Its colour follows the panel: yellow on red or blue,
red on yellow or white. A small word runs vertically up the left edge of it.

Rules, in order of importance:

1. TWO DECIMAL SEPARATORS, ON THE SAME TILE.
   The big number uses a PERIOD: 3.09, 0.99, 12.99. Everything smaller uses a
   COMMA: 5,70 €/kg, La ud sale a 0,66 €. Read each as printed. Never
   normalise one family to the other, and never read 1.29 as 129.
   A trailing dash means no cents: 1.- is 1.00, 7.- is 7.00, and in a
   comparison figure 29,- €/kg is 29.00.

2. "CON LIDL PLUS" IS THE LOYALTY BADGE, AND THE TILE STILL STATES A PRICE
   ANYONE PAYS.
   A yellow flag reading "Con Lidl Plus -NN%" beside the blue "Lidl Plus" logo
   means the big number is the app price. A smaller struck through number to
   its right is the shelf price, which is what a shopper without the app pays,
   and that is the number this reading records:
     - "price"            -> the struck through number, the shelf price.
     - "leaflet.wasPrice" -> null. The struck number is today's price for a
                             non member, not a former price.
     - "unitPrice"        -> the FIRST comparison figure, the struck one,
                             because it is the one that goes with "price".
     - "leaflet.loyalty"  -> false, and "promotion" stays null.
   Set "leaflet.loyalty" to true ONLY when a "Con Lidl Plus" tile prints no
   struck through number at all. Then the leaflet states no price a non member
   can pay, and the builder is right to record no price.

3. A DISCOUNT FLAG WITHOUT THE LIDL PLUS LOGO IS A CUT FOR EVERYBODY.
   A plain "-NN%" flag, a red "Bajada permanente" flag, or an "Oferta" flag,
   each with a struck through number beside the price:
     - "price"            -> the big number.
     - "leaflet.wasPrice" -> the struck through number.
     - "unitPrice"        -> the SECOND comparison figure, the unstruck one.
     - promotion type     -> "price_drop", with the flag's own words in
                             "rawText".
   "Superprecio" with no struck number beside it is a slogan. Leave "promotion"
   and "wasPrice" null.
   THE LIDL PLUS LOGO IS THE ONLY THING THAT SEPARATES RULE 2 FROM RULE 3.
   Reading a Lidl Plus tile as a price drop inverts the offer, and reading a
   plain "-23%" tile as a Lidl Plus one writes the old price as today's.

4. THE VERTICAL WORD BESIDE THE PRICE IS THE BASIS. Decide "leaflet.basis" in
   this order and stop at the first line that matches:
     - The word is "kg", or a yellow tag reads "Precio por kg"   -> "kg"
     - The word is "l"                                           -> "l"
     - The word is "pack", or the size names a multipack or a tray sold
       together: "3 x 370 g", "12 x 100 g", "Pack 3", "Bandeja"  -> "pack"
     - Anything else, including "ud", "1 kg", "500 g",
       "aprox. 300 g" and "2ª ud"                                -> "unit"
   A 1 kg bag of bananas is "unit", because the price buys that one bag. Only a
   price genuinely quoted per kilo, which is loose produce sold "A granel" or a
   sack tagged "Precio por kg", is "kg".

5. THE COMPARISON FIGURES SIT IN THE PRODUCT'S OWN TEXT, NOT IN A BADGE.
   They follow the size: "5,70 €/kg", "3,63 €/l", "2,03 €/100 g",
   "1 ud 7,48 €/kg / 2 uds 5,62 €/kg", "1 a 2 uds 3,75 €/100 g /
   3 uds 2,75 €/100 g".
   When TWO are printed either side of a slash, the first is struck through and
   belongs to the struck number, the second belongs to the big number. Rules 2
   and 3 say which of the two you want. When ONE is printed it belongs to the
   big number.
   The unit price is a price per MEASURE: per kg, per l, per 100 g, per 100 ml.
   When the tile prints one, that is the unit price, even when it also prints
   a "La ud sale a 0,50 €" flag. On a multibuy that flag is the
   "effectiveUnitPrice" of rule 8b, never the unit price. Use a "sale a"
   figure as the unit price ONLY when the tile prints no per measure figure at
   all, as on "El rollo sale a 0,25 €".
   Put the number you chose in "unitPrice", and copy the figure you chose into
   "unitPriceLabel" exactly as printed, comma and abbreviation and all:
     "5,70 €/kg", "3,63 €/l", "2,03 €/100 g", "1,45 €/100 ml",
     "1 ud 9,80 €/kg", "El rollo sale a 0,25 €".
   When two are printed, copy only the one you chose. Never spell the unit out
   and never convert it. When the leaflet misprints its own basis, copy the
   misprint AND say so in "leaflet.doubt": "13,99 €/g" on a 350 g pack stays
   "13,99 €/g", with the doubt "the label says €/g, the figure is per kg".
   A comparison figure is never the product price.

6. "unitSize" AND "sizeFormat" ARE THE SIZE AS A NUMBER AND ITS OWN TOKEN.
   Read them from the size the leaflet prints and keep the leaflet's own unit
   rather than converting it:
     "500 g"          -> unitSize 500,  sizeFormat "g"
     "Aprox. 400 g"   -> unitSize 400,  sizeFormat "g"
     "1 kg"           -> unitSize 1,    sizeFormat "kg"
     "300 ml"         -> unitSize 300,  sizeFormat "ml"
     "1,5 L"          -> unitSize 1.5,  sizeFormat "l"
     "lata 33 cl"     -> unitSize 330,  sizeFormat "ml"
     "3 x 370 g"      -> unitSize 1110, sizeFormat "g"
     "12 x 100 g"     -> unitSize 1200, sizeFormat "g"
     "10 uds. 415 g"  -> unitSize 415,  sizeFormat "g"
     "30 l, 40 bolsas" -> unitSize 30,  sizeFormat "l"
     "A granel"       -> unitSize null, sizeFormat null
   Centilitres are the one conversion you make: 1 cl is 10 ml. A multipack
   states its TOTAL content, so three cartons of 370 g are 1110 g.
   A COUNT IS NOT A SIZE. "40 bolsas", "600 hojas", "80 lavados", "Unidad" and
   "1 ud" leave both null, and the count stays in "leaflet.format". The one
   exception: "ud" is the size when the tile itself compares by the piece, as
   "16 rollos" beside "El rollo sale a 0,25 €" is unitSize 16, sizeFormat
   "ud". When a tile prints a measure as well as a count, the measure is the
   size.
   When a tile prints several sizes for several variants ("50 / 51 / 57 g",
   "390 / 400 g"), set both to null and keep the printed string in
   "leaflet.format", also when rule 15 splits the tile into one row per
   variant. The tile does not say which variant weighs which, so a size per
   variant would be a guess. When the page prints no size at all, set both to
   null. Never guess a size from the photograph.

   "leaflet.format" IS THE PACK AS PRINTED. Keep the count of pieces inside the
   pack ("16 cápsulas, 100,8 g", "12 x 100 g"). Leave out what the product
   does in use ("80 lavados", "40 dosis"): "1,6 l, 80 lavados" is "1,6 l".

7. "price" IS ALWAYS WHAT ONE UNIT COSTS, in whatever basis rule 4 chose.
   On a plain tile that is the big number. On a Lidl Plus tile it is the struck
   shelf price, by rule 2. On a conditional promotion it is the single unit
   price, by rule 8.

8. THE TWO CONDITIONAL PROMOTIONS THIS LEAFLET PRINTS.
   a) SECOND UNIT. A flag reads "-NN% 2ª ud", the vertical word beside the
      price is "2ª ud", and a smaller "1 ud" with a number to the right of the
      price is what one unit costs on its own.
        type              "second_unit_discount"
        rawText           "-50% 2ª ud"
        requiredQuantity  2
        effectiveUnitPrice  null
        singleUnitPrice   the "1 ud" number
        price             the "1 ud" number, because that is what a shopper
                          buying one is charged
        leaflet.wasPrice  null
      Do not put the second unit's number in "price". Do not average the two.
   b) MULTIBUY TOTAL. "Llévate N por X.-", with "La ud sale a Y €" in a flag
      and "1 ud sale a Z" running vertically beside the price.
        type              "multibuy_total"
        rawText           "Llévate 3 por 2.-"
        requiredQuantity  N
        totalPrice        X
        effectiveUnitPrice  Y
        singleUnitPrice   Z
        price             Z
      Read every one of those numbers off the page. Never divide X by N.

9. WHAT IS NOT A PROMOTION. This is the second most common trap after rule 2.
   - "Pack 3", "Pack 12" in a yellow tag on the photograph name the pack size.
     The size the tile states already counts them.
   - "Por tiempo limitado", in a yellow diamond, dates the offer.
   - "Origen España", "Categoría: I.", "Precio por kg", "3 kg", the GGN, MSC,
     ASC, Bienestar Animal and Sabor del Año logos, the "Nº1 CALIDAD PRECIO"
     seal, and the "Karlos Arguiñano" and "Joseba Arguiñano" signatures are
     provenance, not offers.
   - An asterisk after the price points at the page footer, "Artículos
     limitados hasta fin de existencias". It is a stock caveat.
   - Flashes printed on the packaging in the photograph ("5x50g", "Formato
     familiar", "NUEVO") belong to the manufacturer's pack, not to LIDL's
     offer.
   - The blue "¡Dobla tus puntos Lidl! x2" band across the foot of the page
     offers points, not a price. It is not a tile.
   Leave "promotion" null for every one of these.

9b. A NUMBER IN POINTS IS NOT A PRICE, AND ITS TILE IS NOT A ROW.
   The Puntos Lidl pages ("Acumula puntos y llévate premios extra", "1 € = 1
   punto", "Consigue los productos destacados de la semana") show products
   with "92 puntos" under a struck through "185 puntos". Those are rewards the
   app swaps for points, and nobody can buy them for money. Write NO row for
   such a tile: not with the points as the price, and not with a null price.
   A page made only of them returns an empty array. Every price you write is
   in euros, and the word "puntos" beside a number means it is not one.

10. Any other promotion wording maps like this:
    - "2 X 1" or "3 X 2" naming a saving  -> "n_for_m"
    - "LLEVANDO 3, GRATIS 1"              -> "buy_n_get_free"
    - "COMPRANDO 12 UNIDADES"             -> "multibuy_unit_price"
    - "+50% GRATIS", "3+1 GRATIS"         -> "pack_bonus"
    Wording with no old price and no quantity, such as "Vale la pena" or
    "Récord en ahorro", is a slogan. Leave "promotion" null.

11. "categoryPath" IS THE BANNER HEADLINE AT THE TOP OF THE PAGE, copied as
    printed on one line, for example ["Elige la mejor fruta"],
    ["Yo elijo ahorrar con el mejor pescado fresco"], ["Prepara el mejor
    bocadillo"]. Give every product on the page the same path.
    Use an EMPTY ARRAY when:
      - the page prints no banner at all, which is roughly half the pages,
        because LIDL spreads one banner across two pages and only the left page
        carries it; or
      - the banner names no product family but the leaflet, the chain, the app
        or a saving: "Batimos el récord del ahorro", "Récord en ofertas",
        "Récord en precios imbatibles", "Bajadas permanentes", "Súper finde",
        "Tus otras marcas de siempre", "¡Los Nº1 de la semana!", "Elige ahorrar
        después de vacaciones", "Vale la pena", "Tradición que vale la pena",
        "Encuentra la gama que mejor se ajusta", "Bajadas permanentes en 800
        productos".
    Never invent a department the page does not print.

12. Ignore store adverts, app pages, the basket price comparison against
    another supermarket, social media icons, web addresses and legal small
    print. A page that is only an advert returns an empty array, even when it
    prints large numbers. Only include products that carry a price.
    Text printed on the product packaging in the photograph is not the product
    name; use the caption set in the leaflet's own bold text. When the caption
    opens with a brand in capitals ("KANIA", "CHEF SELECT", "MILBONA",
    "REALVALLE", "HARIBO"), that is "brand" and the rest is "name". When the
    caption is nothing but that brand, put it in both. Otherwise the brand
    never appears in "name", not even where the caption repeats it further on:
    "TULGA Vino tinto Tulga D.O. Toro joven" is brand "TULGA", name "Vino tinto
    D.O. Toro joven".
    When the caption is a slash separated list of several brands ("SNICKERS /
    MARS / TWIX / BOUNTY", "MALTESERS / M&M'S"), the list is the "name" and
    "brand" is null: the tile prices whichever of them the shopper picks.
    Keep every word that says WHAT THE PRODUCT IS: the cut, the variety, the
    flavour, the filling, the denomination of origin, the preparation
    ("Contramuslo de pollo deshuesado sin piel", "Pechuga de pavo en finas
    lonchas", "Pasta rellena calabaza y ricotta", "Vino tinto crianza D.O.
    Navarra", "Vino tinto D.O. Ribera del Duero"). Dropping one makes two
    products read as one.
    Leave out every line that SELLS it rather than says what it is, and the
    size: "De calidad suprema", "Ideales para freír", "La ilusión de los más
    pequeños", "Diferentes variedades", "Nubes en forma de árbol de Navidad",
    "3 kg", "en pack de 3". The size belongs in "unitSize" and
    "leaflet.format", never in "name".
    Copy each word letter by letter. Never put a similar ingredient or a more
    familiar word in place of the one printed: "sobrasada" is not "nduja". When
    a word is too small or too blurred to be sure of, write what you see and
    name the word in "leaflet.doubt".

13. AN OFFER'S OWN END DATE. Some tiles print a window of their own, shorter
    than the leaflet's, such as "Oferta válida hasta el 14/09/2026". Copy that
    wording verbatim into "leaflet.validityText" and write the last day it
    names into "leaflet.validUntil" as YYYY-MM-DD: "2026-09-14". When the
    wording prints no year, leave "validUntil" null and still copy the
    wording. "Por tiempo limitado" names no day, so it leaves both null.
    THE STRIP ACROSS THE TOP OF THE PAGE IS NEVER A TILE'S DATE. "Desde el
    lunes 26/10 hasta el 1/11" and "Desde el viernes 30/10 hasta el 1/11" in
    that strip date the whole page, and the import knows them already. Leave
    both null on every tile that prints no date inside its own panel.

14. Use null for anything the page does not state. Do not invent values and do
    not compute a value the page does not print. Never divide one printed
    number by another to produce a price.

15. ONE TILE CAN ADVERTISE SEVERAL PRODUCTS AT ONE PRICE, AND EACH ONE IS A
    ROW. The caption often joins them with "o": "Pan de chorizo o de
    salchichón", "Gel limpiador ácido hialurónico o vitamina C", "Friegasuelos
    olor ropa limpia o bambú", "Zapatilla dinosaurios o estrellas". Decide in
    two steps. First the wording: when the words on each side of the "o" name
    a different product or a different variety, so that the caption makes no
    sense as the name of one product, split it. Then the photograph: when the
    wording still reads as one name, look at the tile's picture; several
    similar packs under one price are several products, and each pack's own
    label says which words belong to which product. Write one row per
    product, each with that product's full name (repeat the shared words:
    "Pan de chorizo Regio", "Pan de salchichón Regio"), and the same price,
    size, unit price, brand, basis and promotion.
    "N variedades:" followed by the varieties IS a split, one row per variety:
    "Piruletas de chocolate. 2 variedades: blanco o con leche" is "Piruletas de
    chocolate blanco" and "Piruletas de chocolate con leche"; "Hummus. 3
    variedades: aceitunas de Kalamata, tomate y albahaca o chili" is three
    rows. The one thing that is NOT a split is a single pack that holds
    several flavours at once, which the size shows: "Surtido", "4 sabores en
    un pack", "3 x 330 ml" of mixed cans. "Diferentes variedades" with no
    variety named is one row.
    The photograph also splits a tile whose caption names no variety at all:
    two packs in the picture, each labelled with its own flavour, are two
    rows named by those labels.
    BEFORE YOU ANSWER, read every "name" you wrote once more. A name that still
    holds " o " between two varieties ("L-Casei natural o fresa", "Champú Color
    Vive o ácido hialurónico") is a tile you forgot to split: write it as two
    rows.

16. "leaflet.doubt" IS WHERE YOU SAY WHAT YOU COULD NOT BE SURE OF. Write one
    plain sentence naming the field and the reason: "the second word of the
    name is blurred, read as sobrasada", "the label says €/g, the figure is per
    kg", "the two packs in the photograph may be one product". Write null on
    every tile you read with confidence, which should be most of them. A doubt
    never replaces a reading or a rule: still fill every field as well as the
    page allows, and by the rules above. When you notice that a tile has no
    Lidl Plus logo, that settles it: rule 3 applies, and the big number is the
    price. A person reads each doubt against the page, so a doubt on the
    right tile saves them reading the whole leaflet.

WHY THREE OF THESE FIELDS MATTER MORE THAN THE REST

The reading is turned into a HarvestDocument (backend plan 0086, section 6.1)
by the shared build-document.mjs, which calls the shared to-harvest-document.mjs.
That script decides which number on a tile a shopper actually pays for one unit,
and it reads exactly three things to decide:

- "leaflet.loyalty". True means the tile states no price at all, because a card
  price is not the price a non member pays. In this leaflet that is rare, and
  rule 2 says why: a LIDL tile usually prints the shelf price beside the app
  price, so the shelf price is what you record and loyalty stays false.
- "leaflet.promotion.type" and "leaflet.promotion.singleUnitPrice". For a second
  unit or a multibuy, the big number is not what one unit costs. The single
  unit price is, and without it the tile states no price.
- "leaflet.basis". A "kg" or "l" basis is a price per weight or volume, so it
  becomes a unit price and the product gets no till price.

Get those three right even when you are unsure about the rest. A wrong price is
worse than a missing one.
